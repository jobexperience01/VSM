How are these numbers calculated?

Takt time = available seconds per day ÷ customer demand per day.

Effective cycle time = cycle time ÷ machines ÷ uptime ÷ yield. The step with the largest value is the bottleneck.

Queue days = WIP before the step ÷ demand per day. Lead time = sum of all queue days.

Process cycle efficiency = value-add time ÷ (lead time × available seconds per day).

Step-by-step: how to run a VSM study
  1. Pick one product family that shares most process steps. Decide where the map starts (supplier or order) and ends (customer shipment).
  2. Get demand and available time. Use units per day and net working minutes per day, after breaks and planned stops. Enter both in Setup.
  3. Walk the flow from customer back to supplier and list each step in order under Process steps. Use the arrows on each step to reorder.
  4. Measure each step on the floor: cycle time per unit (stopwatch, 10 or more cycles), changeover, uptime, yield, machines and operators.
  5. Count the inventory waiting before each step (WIP now).
  6. Classify each step from the customer point of view: value-add, necessary non-value-add or non-value-add.
  7. Read the current-state results: takt, bottleneck, queues, lead time and process cycle efficiency.
  8. Design the future state: set WIP targets, choose FIFO or pull between steps, mark one pacemaker, add kaizen notes.
  9. Play the walkthrough, compare current and future, agree actions, then measure again after the change.
Formulas used in this app
  • Takt time = available time per day (s) ÷ customer demand per day
  • Effective cycle time = cycle time ÷ machines ÷ uptime ÷ yield
  • Daily capacity = available time per day ÷ effective cycle time
  • Queue time (days) = WIP ÷ demand per day. This is Little Law: flow time = WIP ÷ throughput
  • Lead time = sum of queue times. Add curing, transport or other waits as WIP if they must be counted
  • Value-add time = sum of cycle times of value-add steps
  • Process cycle efficiency = value-add time ÷ lead time
  • Bottleneck = step with the largest effective cycle time
